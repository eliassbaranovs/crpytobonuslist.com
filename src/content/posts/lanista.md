### Licensing and Security

Lanista holds licence 0000071 from the Tobique Gaming Commission, a body set up by the Tobique First Nation in New Brunswick, Canada, that has licensed remote gambling since October 2023. It is an offshore-tier regulator alongside Anjouan and the reformed Curacao regime. It requires KYC and, per the Lanista terms, the operator keeps customer funds in segregated client accounts. That segregation clause is a plus. What Tobique does not offer is the binding dispute route you would get from Malta or the UK.

KYC is mandatory. The terms give you 30 days to supply documents once requested, and withdrawals are audited before release. Player reports mention rejected photos and follow-up requests for wallet ownership proof, so upload clean scans early rather than at cashout time.

Complaints go to support first and then to a complaints email with a 10-day resolution target, and the terms point to an external dispute route beyond that. The site is not listed on any major blacklist. The delayed-payout complaints noted above are the main reputational risk, and they are exactly the type of issue that worsens when withdrawal caps force winners into multiple requests.
